The Growth Stages
in Caves

“

A cave is a record of everything that has happened to it. Every drop of water leaves a signature.

Dr. Hazel Barton – Famous Cave Explorer
01

Water Entry

It begins with rain. As water moves through the soil, it picks up acid that eats into the rock. It finds small cracks and slowly makes them bigger, creating the first hidden paths.

02

Stream Flow

This is the phase where the cave really grows. Large amounts of fast-moving water rush through the small cracks. Over time, the water wears down the solid rock, turning narrow gaps into what we see today.

03

Mineral Build-up

Once the water level drops and air fills the cave, every single drop of water falling from the ceiling leaves behind a tiny bit of mineral. These layers build up over hundreds of years to create stone icicles called stalactites and floor-columns called stalagmites.

Physical Structures

The Primary
Formations

01

Stalactites

Stalactites are mineral deposits that hang down from a cave’s ceiling. They are created when water containing dissolved calcium carbonate drips slowly from the roof. As each drop falls, it leaves behind a tiny amount of minerals. Over many years, these mineral rings build up to form a hollow, tapered tube that looks like a stone icicle.

02

Stalagmites

Stalagmites are the thicker, heavy-set structures that grow upward from the cave floor. They form directly underneath stalactites where mineral-rich water drops splash onto the ground. The minerals left behind by these splashes pile up over centuries, creating a solid mound of stone. Unlike their counterparts on the ceiling, stalagmites are usually more rounded and solid throughout.

03

Columns

A column is a solid pillar of stone that connects the floor of a cave to its ceiling. This happens through a process of total intersection, where a stalactite growing down and a stalagmite growing up eventually meet in the middle. Once they touch, the two structures fuse together, creating a single, continuous formation that can support the weight of the cave chamber.

Protection & Sustainability

Protecting the
Caves

Lipids and oils act as a permanent repellent against calcite deposition.

Why Caves are Fragile

Cave formations are among the slowest-growing structures on Earth, taking centuries for a stalactite to grow just one inch, yet seconds to destroy.

Touching a formation leaves behind oils that act as a mineral repellent. This prevents new growth and creates a permanent stain.
